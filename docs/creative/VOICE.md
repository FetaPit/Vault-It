# Vault-It voice

This is how Pete writes, and so how Vault-It sounds. It is drawn from the patterns in his own sent messages (no message content is copied here) and the brand rules in `CLAUDE.md`. Pedro, the creative director, checks all copy against this file. So does any session drafting text for Pete to use elsewhere.

## In one line
Plain, direct and polite. Say the point in the first line, give the concrete detail, then stop.

## How Pete writes
- **Ask first.** The first sentence carries the point or the request. Context follows only if it is needed.
- **Short.** Most messages run two to five lines. One idea per sentence.
- **Concrete.** Exact amounts, times and counts ("a 2 hour slot", "weekly for the next month") rather than adjectives.
- **Polite without fuss.** "Please", "It would be great if you could…", "Happy to jump on a quick call if that's easier." No gushing and no exclamation marks.
- **Honest about gaps.** He says plainly when something isn't clear or he doesn't know.
- **Lists for several asks.** More than one question becomes a short numbered list.
- **One-line pitch.** "A UK app that gives people a live date for owning their home."
- **Sign-off.** "Thanks, Pete" or "Many thanks, Pete Thickett". Greetings are "Hi <name>".

## For product and marketing copy
- Second person: you, your. "Vault-It" with a hyphen.
- British English and sentence case. No em dashes.
- Buttons start with a verb and say what happens: "Join the beta", "Lay a brick", "Show working".
- Numbers beat adjectives: "£50 lays 39 bricks", not "watch your savings grow fast".
- Say limits plainly: "It is not end-to-end encrypted." "Not financial advice."
- No hype words: powerful, seamless, effortless, revolutionary, unlock, supercharge, game-changer.
- No invented proof: no testimonials, user counts, scarcity or reference prices.

## Text Pete will send himself (emails, DMs, posts)
Keep it brief and in the shape above: greeting, the ask or point in line one, the detail, a numbered list if there are several asks, an easy next step, and his sign-off. No emoji in business mail.
