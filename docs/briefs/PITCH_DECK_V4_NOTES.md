## 2. Factored: what the five pages are saying

Grouped by what they change in the deck. The page letter is in brackets.

**A. The spine: Problem, Solution, Result** [D]
- Open by saying what problem we are solving. Spend as much time on the problem as on the product. Say how many people have it: millions, with a number.
- Then "here's why Vault-It's built". The product is the answer to the problem just set up, not a feature list.
- Then the result: what changes for the person, for the partner, and what we have measured so far.

**B. The person before the product** [B, E]
- Talk to the heart. Sell the benefits of the benefits: not "one live date" but what a date does to a Tuesday night when the credit card bill lands.
- Be specific on how this helps the person. Name the saving, the months, the pound figure.
- The Mum Test: would a parent or a mate, not an investor, say "oh, I need that"? If the problem slide does not pass it, rewrite it.
- Can they pay? Answer it on the slide: who pays, how much, and who pays on behalf of those who can't (the Impact licence).

**C. Be specific on competitors** [C, D]
- Name them and say exactly what each does. Moneybox does this (LISA and deposit saving, assumes you have money to put in). Tembo does this (first-time buyer mortgages and LISA, assumes you are mortgage-ready). The gap is below zero and the date.
- "Tripate?" is unread. If it is a competitor, add it to the gap slide. [NEEDS PETE]

**D. Money: revenue stream before business model** [B, E]
- Revenue streams are more important than the model diagram. Lead with where the money comes from, in pounds, then explain the mechanism.
- ROI on effectiveness, tangible: what a user saves or gains by using the app, and what a partner gets per £ of licence.

**E. Positioning** [A]
- Pre-seed, incorporated, pre-revenue. Say it plainly and early: "Vault-It Ltd, registered this week." (v3 says incorporation submitted 6 Nov; confirm the certificate before saying "registered". [NEEDS PETE])
- Equity on offer and a realistic ask: £250k, SEIS advance assurance to follow. [PETE]
- Market validation, waiting list and route to market are the three proof points investors asked for. Each gets a number.
- Support evidence for neurodivergent + finance stays, but after the product, as "who it helps most", not as the opener.

**F. The running visual** [D]
- Slowly build the house during the pitch. Each section lays more bricks, so by the Result section the house is up. Investors see their money translate to bricks and mortar: the user's bricks and the real front door.
- The user's path to home ownership is the same path the slides follow: Stage 0 to keys.

**G. The close** [B, E]
- Close with a call to action. Three asks, each one sentence: the £250k, one introduction to a building society, broker or debt charity, and a mentor.
- Choose a platform and generate a partnership: pick one channel to own (one social platform, or one partner type) rather than listing six.
- Create a conversation script: a short, honest opener for partner conversations (section 7).

**H. Delivery** [C]
- Two versions: a 5-minute A cut and a 10-minute B cut (section 6).
- Record every run-through. Watch it for body language and flow.
- Structure every sentence: one idea, a verb, under 20 words. Say the number, then what it means.
- Audience contract: tell them at the outset what they will get in the next five minutes and what you will ask at the end. Assume patience: do not rush the problem. Reward them: the house building, the live 3D, the date.
- Things we love: say what you love about the product and the people it is for. Investors back conviction.

## 7. Partner conversation script

For the first call with a building society, broker or debt charity. Short on purpose: it is a conversation, not a pitch. Mum Test rules: ask about their life before Vault-It is mentioned.

1. "What happens to someone who comes to you two years before they're mortgage-ready? Where do they go?"
2. "How do you find out when they're ready?"
3. "If you could see a saver's progress, with their permission, from the day they decide to buy, what would you do differently?"
4. "I've built something that shows people one date for owning a home, starting from debt. 26 people are using it now. Can I show you for two minutes?"
5. "Would a 90-day pilot with one success measure be worth £2,500 to you? If not, what would make it worth it?"
6. "Who else here should see this?"
