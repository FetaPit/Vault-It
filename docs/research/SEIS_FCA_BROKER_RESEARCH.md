# SEIS, FCA costs and the broker and mortgage idea

Owner ask, 11 Nov 2026: "research into SEIS registration, Cost FCA Reg costs. Plus how Vault-it could help reduction in time and risk for brokers and mortgage, 25% more into affordability."

Research only. Not legal, tax or financial advice. A solicitor, an accountant and an FCA consultant confirm before anyone acts. Figures come from the pages linked at the end, read on 11 Nov 2026. Where a figure is from general knowledge or a vendor, it says so.

## 1. SEIS (Seed Enterprise Investment Scheme)

**What it is.** A scheme that gives UK taxpayers tax relief for buying new shares in very early companies, so the company can raise money more easily.

**Company tests (gov.uk, updated 6 April 2026):**
- Maximum raised in its lifetime under SEIS: **£250,000**.
- Less than **3 years old** at the time of investment.
- No more than **£350,000 in gross assets**.
- Fewer than **25 employees**.
- Has not previously carried on a different trade.
- UK permanent establishment, plans to spend the money on a qualifying trade, not listed, not controlled by another company.
- Not eligible if it has already had investment through EIS or a VCT.

**Investor side (general knowledge, confirm on gov.uk):** 50% income tax relief on the amount invested, up to an annual investor limit, plus capital gains reliefs. Investors must hold the shares for a minimum period. This is why investors like it and why founders can use it as a selling point.

**The catch for Vault-It: the trade must qualify.** A company may not qualify if more than 20% of its trade is in excluded areas. The list on gov.uk includes "legal or financial services" and "banking, insurance, debt or financing services".
- Vault-It today is a budgeting and planning app, sold as a software subscription, not regulated (it says "Not regulated by the FCA. Not financial advice."). Whether HMRC sees that as a qualifying trade is a question for advance assurance, not something to assume.
- **If Vault-It became an FCA-authorised mortgage broker or adviser, that trade would very likely count as financial services and put SEIS at risk.** The broker route and SEIS pull against each other. Decide which first.

**Process (typical, confirm with HMRC):**
1. Company is set up and trading intent is clear (the entity name is still open, see the trust centre plan).
2. Apply to HMRC for **advance assurance** with a business plan, share structure and cap table. HMRC says assurance covers only certain conditions and is not an endorsement. Third parties quote 4 to 6 weeks for a reply; that is not an official figure.
3. Raise the money and issue shares.
4. Submit the compliance statement (SEIS1) to HMRC, then HMRC issues SEIS3 certificates to investors, who claim relief.
- **Cost:** HMRC charges nothing. Fees come from a solicitor (share structure, articles, investment agreement) and an accountant or tax adviser for the assurance pack. I did not find a reliable price; get two quotes.
- **Coming change:** from 6 April 2027 a SEIS and an EIS or VCT investment can happen on the same day, and SEIS interest on withdrawn relief is aligned with EIS (gov.uk, 28 Oct 2026). Not relevant for a first raise, but good to know for a later one.

**Why it is worth checking now:** it is the cheapest route for angel money in the UK, and the December pitch is the date Vanta pointed at. Advance assurance takes weeks, so start before the pitch if the company entity is settled.

## 2. FCA costs

**Application fees (FCA page, last updated 3 Nov 2026).** Fees run from £280 (Category 1) to £225,170 (Category 10). The ones that matter:

| What | Category | Fee |
|---|---|---|
| Most financial advisers, mortgage brokers and general insurance intermediaries | 4 | **£2,820** |
| Registered account information service provider (RAISP, reading bank data under open banking) | 3 | **£1,130** |
| Small payment institution or small e-money institution | 3 | £1,130 |
| Consumer credit, straightforward full permission | 3 (or 5, 6 by permission) | £1,130 or more |
| Consumer credit, limited permission | 2 | £560 |
| Consumer buy-to-let arranger or adviser, with Part 4A permission | 1 | £280 |

- The application fee is separate from the **annual (periodic) fee**, which I did not retrieve. It depends on the firm's size and activities. Check FEES 4.
- **Time (general knowledge, FSMA):** the FCA has up to 6 months for a complete application and 12 months for an incomplete one. Consultancies quote 6 to 15 months in practice.
- **Other costs the fee does not cover:** a compliance consultant or law firm to prepare the application (low five figures is a common consultant quote, unverified), professional indemnity insurance, compliance staff and systems, approved persons or senior manager arrangements, and capital requirements that depend on the permission. I did not find a trustworthy single figure for a small mortgage broker. Ask three consultants for fixed quotes.

**The appointed representative (AR) route.** Instead of applying directly, a firm becomes an appointed representative of an authorised "principal" firm, and works under that firm's permission.
- Faster: vendors quote weeks (2 to 8 weeks is one figure), not months.
- No FCA application fee for the AR itself. The principal charges its own fees, which vary and which I could not find a typical range for. Principals take on responsibility for the AR, so they do checks and ask for commission or fees.
- Common pattern: start as an AR to prove the model, move to direct authorisation later. The principal's rules limit what the AR can do and how it can market.
- Mortgage-specific AR terms (network fees, lender panel access) were not covered by what I found. Ask mortgage networks directly.

**What Vault-It would actually need depends on what it does:**

| If Vault-It... | Likely regulated activity | Rough application cost |
|---|---|---|
| Stays a budgeting tool, no advice, no brokering | None. This is where it is now | £0 |
| Reads bank data itself through open banking | Account information service. Most apps avoid this by using a regulated partner's connection instead | £1,130 (Category 3) if registered itself |
| Sells software to brokers (they use it, Vault-It does not advise or arrange) | Probably none for Vault-It, but check, see below | £0 |
| Passes users to brokers for a fee or commission | May be "arranging" or introducing. Needs a legal view | AR route first, or £2,820 (Category 4) |
| Advises on or arranges mortgages itself | Mortgage advising and arranging | £2,820 (Category 4) plus consultants, PI insurance, capital, conduct rules |

Vanta's Cian Whyte also said, in writing, that Vanta does not help with FCA authorisation and that it is a separate project with a separate budget (see the trust centre plan).

## 3. How Vault-It could help brokers and mortgage lenders

**What brokers and lenders already do.** Mortgage firms must assess affordability responsibly (FCA rules in MCOB 11). In practice that means collecting payslips and bank statements, finding commitments and expenditure, and re-working cases when something is missing or wrong. Brokers describe the time spent chasing documents and finding problems late.

**What is on the market (all vendor claims, not independent proof):**
- Afford.Mortgage: open banking affordability snapshots for brokers, to spot problems early and cut rejections (trade press, June to July 2025).
- Mortgage Assist Pro: AI document and bank statement checks, claims 30 to 40 minutes saved per case in one testimonial, and "up to 70%" of document review time in marketing copy.
- Sikoia with broker Y3S: extracts income and risk indicators from statements, payslips and tax returns, "saving over an hour per application" (Financial Reporter, Jan 2025).
- Experian Affordability Passport: open banking income and expenditure for lenders, claims up to 75% underwriting efficiency and 95% categorisation accuracy.
- Credit Canary, Apply24: open banking evidence for brokers, claims faster cases and fewer declines.
- An agency case study showing "2.8 hours to 45 minutes" is anonymous with a withheld name. Do not quote it.

So the idea is not new, and the broker-side market has players with FCA-regulated bank connections. Vault-It should not pretend to compete on verified bank evidence.

**Where Vault-It could be different.** Vault-It sits with the **buyer**, before the broker is involved. A user who has filled in the housing and expenditure form, with their deposit plan and debts, could hand a broker a clean, structured "mortgage readiness pack". Possible benefits to a broker, as hypotheses to test:
- Less time on the first fact-find: the user arrives with income, commitments, deposit and target price already organised.
- Fewer surprises: debts and commitments are listed up front.
- Better prepared clients, who are further from a decline.
- The pack is **user-entered, not verified**. The lender still needs its own evidence. Vault-It must say so on every pack. It must not call figures "verified".

**Time and risk reductions that could be measured honestly:** minutes saved on the first fact-find, number of missing-information chasers per case, number of cases reworked after a late-found commitment. None has been measured for Vault-It. A small pilot with two or three brokers would produce real numbers.

**The "25% more into affordability" idea.** I found **no evidence** that clearer or verified data lifts what a lender will lend by 25%, and I could not source the figure. Do not use it anywhere until it has evidence behind it.
- Verified data can move assessed affordability up or down. Lenders must lend responsibly, so a claim that a tool gets people "25% more" would push the wrong way: it reads like a promise of more borrowing, which is a financial promotion and Consumer Duty risk, and it clashes with the standing rule not to guess figures.
- A claim you can safely make once tested is about **time and fewer reworks**, not about borrowing more.
- If the owner's 25% comes from a conversation, a lender or a broker, get it in writing with the source, and treat it as a hypothesis.

## 4. What to do next (suggested order)

1. Settle the company entity (already an owner decision in the trust centre plan). SEIS, the MSA and any FCA application all hang on it.
2. Ask an accountant or SEIS-experienced solicitor: would Vault-It's trade qualify for SEIS, and what changes if it later brokers mortgages. Ask for a fixed quote for advance assurance.
3. Ask an FCA consultant: does selling a tool to brokers, or passing users to brokers, need any permission? Ask for fixed quotes for direct authorisation and the AR route, and for the annual fee and capital for each.
4. Run a small broker pilot of the readiness pack to get real time-saved numbers, with no advice and no "verified" wording.
5. Update the FCA decision in `docs/plans/TRUST_CENTRE_ISO27001_PLAN.md` (decision 8) once 2 and 3 are answered.
