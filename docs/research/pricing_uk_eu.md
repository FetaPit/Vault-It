# UK / EU pricing comparables for Vault·It (net-worth / house-deposit PFM app)

Prepared 2026-10-27. Every figure below carries a source URL. "Retrieved" = 2026-10-27 unless a different "as-of" date is given. Owner's rule applied: no guessed figures. Anything I could not confirm from a page I opened is marked UNVERIFIED or listed under Gaps. Items labelled DERIVED are my own arithmetic on sourced figures. Items labelled INFERENCE are judgement, not fact.

## 0. How this was researched (read first)

- The built-in WebFetch tool failed for every host (DNS error `ENOTFOUND`), and direct `curl` was blocked by the session egress proxy (403 on CONNECT). I therefore read pages through the Exa search/fetch tools, which return text from Exa's crawl of the official pages (crawl date is not always shown) plus current third-party articles.
- Consequence: where a figure comes from an official page I say "official". Where the official page was not retrievable or showed stale/expired content, I say so and rely on dated third-party articles (all checked in Jul–Oct 2026) and mark the row UNVERIFIED or "official + secondary".
- Web content is untrusted data; I ignored any instructions inside pages.
- Currency: GBP for UK, EUR for EU apps. Where a vendor only publishes USD (Spendee, PocketSmith, YNAB) I give USD and do not convert.

## 1. Headline findings (all supported below)

1. UK paid budgeting/PFM apps cluster at £3.99–£5.99 a month for the entry paid tier and £9.99–£14.99 for the "everything" tier. Entry tiers: Plum Plus £3.99, Emma Plus £4.99, Snoop Plus £5.99 (Wallet Premium £5.49 on the UK App Store). Top tiers: Emma Pro £9.99, Cleo Pro £12.99, Emma Ultimate £14.99, Plum Max £14.99.
2. Net-worth tracking is a paid-tier feature in the UK apps that offer it: Snoop Plus (£5.99/mo, £47.99/yr) and Emma Pro (£9.99/mo, £83.99/yr; not in Emma Plus). WPS LifeStage (Moneyhub successor, £1.49/mo, £14.99/yr) runs on Moneyhub's platform, which had net worth, but I could not confirm the feature on WPS's own pages. It is FREE (with limits) at the EU investor-tracker apps getquin and Finary.
3. Annual discount in the UK leaders is about 30–33% (Emma 30%, Snoop 33%); smaller UK/EU players give 16–25% (Plum 18.5–24.9%, Chip 16.5%, WPS/Moneyhub 16%). EU/US-priced apps often give more (Bankin' Plus 33%, Finary Plus 50%, Spendee Premium 50%, YNAB 39%).
4. Consumer-PFM is a hard business in the UK: Money Dashboard shut its apps on 31 Oct 2023 ("could not find a sustainable business model"); Moneyhub closed its consumer app on 31 Jul 2026. Survivors monetise by paywalling bank connections (Emma), referral/switching plus a parent bank (Snoop/Vanquis), bundling into a bank plan (Monzo Extra £3), or selling savings/investing with a subscription on top (Plum, Chip, Moneybox).
5. Best UK evidence of willingness to pay: Monzo reports 1.6m paying subscribers = 16% of monthly active users (FY2026 annual report). Emma's CEO says roughly half its users pay (self-reported, interview). I found NO UK survey that directly measures willingness to pay for a budgeting or net-worth app (see section 8).
6. Price drift is upward: Snoop Plus rose from £4.99/£39.99 to £5.99/£47.99 (by 11 Dec 2025); Monzo Perks £7→£9 and Max £17→£19 (Aug–Oct 2026); Revolut Premium/Metal/Ultra rose between 25 Jul and 21 Oct 2026; getquin raised prices ~80–100% in 2023.
7. Lifetime deals are NOT offered by the UK/EU leaders sampled (Emma, Snoop, Plum, Chip, Finary, getquin, Monzo, Moneybox). Found only at low-cost apps: Wallet (UK App Store "Lifetime Premium £24.99") and Spendee (Lifetime Premium €129.99 on one App Store listing; older SKUs €49.99/€69.99); PocketGuard (US) $149.99.

## 6. Closures and consolidation (why UK consumer PFM pricing looks the way it does)

- Money Dashboard: both consumer apps closed 31 Oct 2023, accounts and bank connections removed; company said it "could not find a sustainable business model for the apps" and moved to B2B open banking (official https://www.moneydashboard.com/about). ClearScore had acquired it in 2022 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money_Dashboard, https://emma-app.com/blog/now-that-money-dashboard-has-closed-what-are-my-budgeting-app-alternatives).
- Moneyhub: announced Feb 2025 it was winding down its direct-to-consumer app (https://tech.eu/2025/02/14/moneyhub-scraps-d2c-app-over-30-jobs-reported-to-be-axed/). Earlier communications said 14 Aug 2026 (https://moneytothemasses.com/news/best-moneyhub-alternatives); its latest help-centre notice (updated 15 Oct 2026) says the app closed on 31 July 2026 and unmigrated accounts were deleted, with no data recovery (https://moneyhubhelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/48275693667217-Moneyhub-App-Closure-and-Your-Account-Options). Users could migrate to WPS LifeStage (price in section 2). Source disagreement on the date: I use 31 Jul 2026 (the company's most recent statement). Moneyhub's low price (£1.49/mo) did not sustain a consumer business; do not treat it as a floor to copy.
- Cleo relaunched in the UK in Feb 2026 with a chat-led model and Pro at £12.99/mo (sources in section 2).

## 7. Open Banking subscription landscape (UK)

- Scale: Open Banking Limited reports "more than 17.9 million user connections" live (release dated 2026-05-07, https://www.openbanking.org.uk/news/obl-further-strengthens-board-with-appointment-of-sean-martin-as-ined/) and "more than 19 million active user connections" in the June 2026 fraud monitor (published 2026-07-08, https://www.openbanking.org.uk/insights/open-banking-payments-fraud-monitor-june-2026-edition/). Caveat from OBL: connections are counted per bank brand and are not deduplicated across brands, so these are NOT unique people (https://www.openbanking.org.uk/insights/open-banking-in-2025-now-part-of-the-uks-everyday-financial-life/, 2026-01-29). Earlier OBL Impact Report 7: 13.3 million active users (individuals and small businesses) in March 2025, "1 in 5" of consumers and small businesses (https://www.openbanking.org.uk/insights/obl-impact-report-7-open-banking-delivers-real-world-impact-as-adoption-accelerates-year-on-year/, 2025-05-16). OBL also lists 145 live third-party providers and noted 33 left the market since 2022 (same report).
- Consumer app use (older): OBL's 2021 Marketing Means survey (n=4,014 UK adults 16+, fieldwork 25 Aug–14 Sep 2021) found 11.6% claimed to use one of nine named open-banking apps; PFM apps were 36% of that user set; PFM-app NPS +25.6 (https://www.openbanking.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/Open-Banking-third-party-provider-customer-survey-Nov-2021-Marketing-Means-v1.pdf). Treat as 2021 data.
- Business models that survive (sourced above): (a) Emma: paywall on bank connections and budgeting; CEO says it took four years to find the revenue model and the turning point was "paywalling the bank connections themselves"; free tier limited to 2 accounts; seven-day free trial; paid social acquisition with a quick payback; revenue about 90% subscription, 10% affiliate (interview, Fintech Growth Insider, 2026-07-02, https://www.fintechgrowthinsider.com/p/edoardo-moreni-emma). (b) Snoop: free app funded by switching commissions inside a bank group (Vanquis describes it as an "efficient acquisition channel"). (c) Monzo/Starling: tools bundled in a bank; Monzo charges £3 for multi-bank view. (d) Plum/Chip/Moneybox: savings and investing margin plus subscription. (e) Wallet/Spendee/PocketSmith/YNAB: pure subscription with bank sync as the paid unlock.
- Cost driver (INFERENCE from the above, not a sourced cost figure): every surveyed UK/EU app that offers live bank sync either charges for it (Emma, Spendee, Wallet, PocketSmith, Finary beyond 2 syncs) or funds it from another revenue line (Snoop, Monzo, Starling). I did not find a published per-connection cost for UK Open Banking aggregators.
- Relevance to Vault·It (INFERENCE; I did not inspect the app's data-entry model, and the task does not state whether it uses live bank sync): if the product does NOT depend on live bank sync (manual/CSV net worth), it avoids the main cost that forces the competitors to charge, but it also lacks the "automatic" selling point the paid unlock rests on at Emma/Snoop. getquin and Finary give a net-worth overview free and charge for analytics instead.

## 10. Patterns I can support for the UK band

All patterns are drawn from the sourced tables above; ranges are list prices at 2026-10-27.

1. Entry paid tier (monthly): £3.99 (Plum Plus), £4.99 (Emma Plus), £5.49 (Wallet, UK App Store), £5.99 (Snoop Plus). Band: about £4–£6 a month.
2. Net-worth-capable paid tier (UK, confirmed): £5.99/mo and £47.99/yr (Snoop Plus); £9.99/mo and £83.99/yr (Emma Pro). WPS LifeStage at £1.49/mo and £14.99/yr (the closed Moneyhub's platform) is a low-price outlier whose net-worth feature I could not confirm on WPS's pages. Net worth sits above the entry tier at Emma; at Snoop it is the same single paid tier.
3. Top "everything" tier: £9.99–£14.99 a month (Emma Pro/Ultimate, Plum Max, Cleo Pro £12.99), with the higher prices justified by bundled money value (savings-rate uplifts, investing fee cuts, credit tools, perks), not by the budgeting alone.
4. Annual price: 8.0–8.4 months of the monthly price at the two UK category leaders (Emma 30%, Snoop 33%). Smaller UK players 9.0–10.9 months (16–25% off). Annual list prices for entry/net-worth tiers: £41.99–£47.99 (Emma Plus, Snoop Plus); £83.99 (Emma Pro).
5. Annual discount: UK range 16–33%; the UK leaders sit at 30–33%; EU/US-priced apps often 33–50%. A 30% discount is the best-supported UK default.
6. Free tier is the norm (Emma, Snoop, Plum, Chip, Moneybox, Finary, getquin, Spendee, Wallet, PocketSmith, Bankin', Monzo, Starling). Exceptions: YNAB (34-day no-card trial only) and WPS LifeStage (30-day trial only). Net worth specifically is gated in UK apps (Snoop Plus, Emma Pro) but free in the investor apps (getquin, Finary).
7. Trials: 7 days (Emma, Snoop Plus, Spendee), 14 days (Finary Plus/Pro, Parqet), first month free (Plum paid), 28 days (ChipX, secondary), 30 days (WPS), 34 days no card (YNAB).
8. Lifetime pricing: none among the UK/EU leaders. Low-cost apps list lifetime at about £25 (Wallet, UK App Store) up to €129.99 (Spendee, one listing); PocketGuard US $149.99 (from a US review site, https://moneynerd.co.uk/best-budgeting-apps/ checked 4 Oct 2026).
9. Bank-bundled budgeting is cheap: Monzo Extra £3/mo (£36/yr) is the cheapest well-known paid multi-bank view; Starling's insights are free.
10. Direction of travel: list prices are rising (Snoop Plus in 2025; Monzo Perks/Max and Revolut Premium/Metal/Ultra in 2026; getquin in 2023) rather than falling. I found no evidence of a UK budgeting-app list-price cut.
