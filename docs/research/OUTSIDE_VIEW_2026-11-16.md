## 4. Do institutions pay small fintechs?

- COMPANY, Nationwide 23 Apr 2019: Open Banking for Good had "a £3 million fund". Seven firms were chosen "from more than 50 applicants".
- RESEARCH, University of Bristol evaluation, Mar 2021: five firms finished. Three deployed "at scale through partnerships with financial services firms and energy companies". One firm's "major challenge" was "finding a sustainable business model that could generate a revenue stream", because its users could not pay.
- RESEARCH, same report: Tully ran a 221-user pilot with HSBC in spring 2020, after which "HSBC's Financial Fitness Team chose to implement Money Coaching as a third-party product". No price disclosed.
- PRESS, Jul 2020: the Money and Pensions Service chose OpenWrks' MyBudget for a debt-advice pilot. Later "deployed by more than 10 organisations".
- PRESS, 15 Dec 2020: Leeds Building Society adopted Paylink's Embark for borrowers in difficulty. No price disclosed.
- FILING via aggregator: Moneyhub turnover £5,502,348 and loss £12,791,021 (year to 28 Feb 2025). Bud turnover £3,211,680 and pre-tax loss £10,136,224 (year to Nov 2025).
- PRESS, Computer Weekly 13 Nov 2019: employers pay Wagestream "about £1 per employee per month".
- OFFICIAL, Fair4All Finance 11 Jul 2024: £3.4m for ClearScore's debt-consolidation technology, with a revenue share.
- Sales cycles: 56% of buyers report 6 to 12 months (VENDOR survey of large firms, Nov 2025). A "two-year contracting and onboarding process" is "not uncommon" for small vendors (industry paper, 2023). Pilots "typically" run "eight to 12 weeks" (VENDOR).
- Broker leads: £5 to £40 a lead (VENDOR price lists). Lenders pay brokers "around 0.35 to 0.45%" (COMPANY, Mojo).
- Not found: any UK building society paying a start-up per active user, any disclosed pilot price, any stated security requirement for these deals.

## 6. What this suggests (to test, not to trust)

1. Standalone consumer money apps in the UK have mostly failed to pay their way. The ones still running sit inside a lender or a broker as a customer funnel, or charge for something beyond budgeting.
2. Nude is the closest comparable: first-home savers, a small monthly fee, about 20,000 customers, about £5m raised, then absorbed by a broker.
3. Reaching 100,000 users took the 2016 to 2017 survivors one to two years, driven by press, waitlists and crowdfunding. Later entrants were slower and their spikes came from one-off events.
4. Institutions paid for narrow, embedded tools after a pilot with one named internal team. Programme and grant money funded the early stage more clearly than contracts did.
5. No public price exists for the B2B offers in our plan, so every B2B figure stays an assumption until a partner names one.
