## 1. Bottom line (read this first)

1. **Rewards-based / pre-sale money is outside FCA regulation; equity is not.** The FCA says donation-based and "rewards-based or pre-payment" crowdfunding are not regulated by it, whereas loan-based and investment-based crowdfunding are ([FCA, Understanding crowdfunding, published 2026-05-27](https://www.fca.org.uk/consumers/crowdfunding), P). Vault·It can take pre-sale money for a product/service. It must not offer anything that looks like a financial return (see 3.4).
2. **Kickstarter is possible but not risk-free for a live, paid, subscription app.** Apps are allowed ("App Projects ... a prototype must be demonstrated") and Technology is a listed category, but Kickstarter also bans "Equity", "Service Providers" ("Projects cannot be centered around establishing and providing services"), requires rewards to be new, and says it does not permit "offering something that's already available somewhere else as a project's end goal" ([Kickstarter Rules](https://www.kickstarter.com/rules), P; [Kickstarter Help, Can Kickstarter be used to fund anything?](https://help.kickstarter.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005134333-Can-Kickstarter-be-used-to-fund-anything), P). Vault·It already has a live beta and live Stripe payment links, so the campaign must fund a **new, defined deliverable** (see 3.1).
3. **A "Founding Member" pre-sale through Stripe is workable** but sits next to Stripe's *restricted* "Crowdfunding and fundraising" category ("businesses offering a reward in return for donation") ([Stripe, Prohibited and Restricted Businesses, last updated 2026-09-22](https://stripe.com/gb/legal/restricted-businesses), P). Describe it as a sale of a licence with a defined deliverable, not as "donations/backing". The 14-day cancellation right (CCR 2013 regs 29-37) and, for subscription contracts from **1 Jan 2027**, the DMCC Act subscription rules apply (see 3.5).
4. **Ads: a tracker is not a regulated financial product, but platforms' classifiers and definitions can still catch it.** Google's policy defines financial products and services as "related to the management or investment of money and cryptocurrencies, including personalised advice" ([Google Ads, Financial products and services](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/2464998?hl=en-GB), P); Reddit expressly **exempts** "personal finance software" from its sales-managed financial restriction ([Reddit, Financial/Cryptocurrency Products and Services](https://business.reddithelp.com/s/article/financial-cryptocurrency-products-and-services-policy), P). FCA-authorisation gates (Google UK, TikTok UK, LinkedIn UK, Meta UK) are aimed at firms promoting regulated financial services. Plan for a classification dispute and use the "non-financial services advertiser" route on Google (see 4.2).
5. **The real FCA perimeter risks are in Vault·It's own copy and features, not in the ad platform.** (a) A personalised "your payoff order" that steers a user's repayment of consumer-credit debts can be **debt counselling** under Article 39E RAO (PERG 17.5 Q5.5, Q5.7; PERG 17.7 examples 15-16) unless it is neutral, balanced information the user drives. (b) A Vault·It-provided Open Banking "dashboard" is an **account information service** needing FCA registration (PERG 15 Q25A). (c) Any public "invest in Vault·It" message is a **financial promotion** (s21 FSMA, criminal offence). See 4.1.
6. **Creative rules that will actually get ads rejected:** no "Are you in debt?" / "Struggling with £X debt?" (Meta Personal Attributes bans implying knowledge of "vulnerable financial status" and "personal or organizational financial information"); no debt/income/net-worth fields in Meta lead forms; no guarantees or unrealistic outcomes; 18+ targeting; landing page must match the ad.
7. **Pixels and ad tags need prior opt-in consent in the UK.** ICO: "The use of storage and access technologies for online advertising purposes requires consent", including ad **measurement** ([ICO, How do the rules apply to online advertising?](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-the-use-of-storage-and-access-technologies/how-do-the-rules-apply-to-online-advertising), P). The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 added an analytics exception (in force **5 Feb 2026**) but it explicitly does **not** cover advertising. PECR fines rose from £500,000 to **£17.5m or 4% of worldwide turnover** (same date).
8. **Simplest compliant launch design:** no Meta Pixel / Google Ads tag on `/sign-up` at first. Use UTM-tagged links, first-party sign-up counts and a cookieless aggregate analytics tool, with a plain-English notice and an opt-out link (needed for the statistical-purposes exception). Add a proper consent banner (Reject as prominent as Accept) only when you switch retargeting on.
9. **Email list:** marketing consent must be a separate, unticked, specific opt-in (PECR reg 22; the "soft opt-in" is only for people who have bought from you). Do not ask people to enter friends' emails. A data-protection complaints procedure has been a legal duty since 19 Jun 2026.
10. **Existing `landing.html` items to fix before copying anything to `/sign-up`** (verified by reading the repo read-only): "Limited to 33 spots" and testimonials must be provably true; "I built every line of this myself" conflicts with AI-disclosure rules if AI wrote code; Google Fonts and jsDelivr are third-party requests; the privacy line says "we won't send marketing unless you opt in" which must match whatever the new form does. See section 2.

## 3. Crowdfunding and early-money rules

### 3.1 Kickstarter

**Verdict:** A software/app project is allowed in principle; a personal-finance niche is not itself prohibited; but Vault·It must pass four tests (new deliverable, not a "service provider" project, no equity/returns, honest prototype demo). Ask Kickstarter before building the campaign (their own help page says "If you're unsure whether your idea would make a good fit for Kickstarter, feel free to get in touch").

#### Creator requirements for a UK creator ([Kickstarter Help, Who can use Kickstarter? updated 2026-08-06](https://help.kickstarter.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005128594-What-are-the-fees), P)

- United Kingdom is on the supported-country list.
- Individuals must be **18+**; to raise funds as an individual you "must be a resident of the country where you launch" and hold a "government issued ID from that same country".
- As an entity: launch in the country where it is registered; the person setting it up must be an owner/executive; "All beneficial owners ... must also be disclosed and verified"; you need "government issued IDs and tax documents for the primary entity".
- "You need a supported bank account located in the same country ... owned by and in the name of the individual or entity raising funds" and "a current account that supports direct deposits in the currency in which the funds are being raised".
- A major credit/debit card in the raiser's name is also required.
- Review: first-time creators submit a basic draft; "Project review typically takes 3-6 business days" ([Kickstarter, 5 Ways to Maximize Pre-Launch Sign-Ups, 2024-12-02](https://updates.kickstarter.com/how-to-maximize-pre-launch-sign-ups-for-your-kickstarter-campaign/), P).
- Duration: "anywhere from 1 day, to a maximum of 60 days"; Kickstarter recommends "30 days or less" ([Help, maximum project duration, 2026-08-06](https://help.kickstarter.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005128434-What-are-the-guidelines-for-a-project-s-funding-duration), P).
- Reward tier limits for UK-based projects: minimum **£1**, maximum **£8,000** ([Help, reward tier value, 2026-08-06](https://help.kickstarter.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005128334-Is-there-a-maximum-reward-tier-value-), P).

#### Fees ([Kickstarter, Fees: United Kingdom](https://www.kickstarter.com/help/fees?country=GB), P; page undated)

- "If your project is successfully funded ... Kickstarter's 5% fee and payment processing fees (between 3% and 5%). Please note we do not withhold VAT. If funding isn't successful, there are no fees."
- Payment processing: **3% + £0.30 per pledge**; pledges under £10: **5% + £0.08**.
- **Disagreement:** secondary pages still quote "3% + £0.20" and "5% + £0.05" ([Adam Webb, 2026-07-07](https://adamwebb.pro/blog/kickstarter-fees-explained-with-calculator/), S; [Kickstarter UK launch post, 2012](https://www.kickstarter.com/blog/kickstarter-in-the-uk), P but 2012). The official fees page, opened today, says £0.30 / £0.08, so use those.
- **Illustrative arithmetic (I)** from the published rates, before failed-payment losses, tax and exchange: a £20 pledge costs £1.00 + £0.60 + £0.30 = £1.90 (9.5%); £49.99 costs £2.50 + £1.50 + £0.30 = £4.30 (8.6%); £199 costs £9.95 + £5.97 + £0.30 = £16.22 (8.2%).

### 3.4 Equity crowdfunding and what Vault·It must NOT do (if it only wants rewards/pre-sale money)

**Platforms:**
- **Crowdcube:** raise only for a "UK or Irish limited company (not an LLP or Sole Trader)" with a Companies House number ([Crowdcube Help, Can I apply?](https://help.crowdcube.com/hc/en-us/articles/206232424-Can-I-apply-to-raise-finance-on-Crowdcube), P). Minimum raise "£150k/€150k" ([Crowdcube Help](https://help.crowdcube.com/hc/en-us/articles/115001549264-How-much-can-I-raise), P). Fees: listing fee "£4,995 ... Focus Raise" to "£9,995 ... Full Access", success fee 5% (Focus) / "up to 8%" (Full Access), plus a 2.5% platform fee and an annual nominee fee of £750 or £1,000 ([Crowdcube Help, fees](https://help.crowdcube.com/hc/en-us/articles/206232464-What-fees-does-Crowdcube-charge-for-raising-finance-on-the-platform), P). Pitches are "a financial promotion" and Crowdcube verifies material statements ([Crowdcube Help, Raising finance through equity](https://help.crowdcube.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000035070-Raising-finance-through-equity), P).
- **Republic Europe (formerly Seedrs, rebranded 10 July 2024):** FCA-regulated platform ([Republic Europe, rebrand note, 2024-07-09](https://europe.republic.com/insights/our-news/seedrs-becomes-republic-europe-investor-guide), P). Company-side fee terms I could open were an older campaign T&C page (URL ends `_old`): 6% success fee + £/€2,500 admin fee + processing fees ([Republic Europe Campaign T&Cs (old)](https://europe.republic.com/pages/campaign-ts-cs_old), P but possibly superseded). Typical minimum target about £/€150,000 ([Company Signal](https://www.companysignal.co.uk/funding/republic-europe), S).
- Market-cost context: an independent estimate puts platform charges on a £300,000 Crowdcube Full Access raise at roughly £43,000 and legal costs at £3,000-£15,000+ ([BusinessExpert, 2026-04-29](https://www.businessexpert.co.uk/alternative-finance/equity-crowdfunding-for-business/), S; their figures are labelled estimates).
- Conclusion (I): equity crowdfunding needs an incorporated company, six figures to be worthwhile, and heavy compliance. It is not a "pre-sign-up" tool.

**Law that makes public equity offers dangerous:**
- **s21 FSMA financial promotion restriction.** "A person must not, in the course of business, communicate an invitation or inducement to engage in investment activity" unless authorised, approved by an authorised person, or exempt. The FCA says financial promotions "can take the form of a website, Facebook post, tweet, etc." ([FCA, Regulating Financial Promotions and Adverts](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/financial-promotions-adverts), P); the restriction "applies even where a communication originates outside the UK if it is capable of having an effect in the UK", and "A breach of s21 is a criminal offence which is punishable by up to 2 years imprisonment, the imposition of an unlimited fine, or both" ([FCA FG24/1, paras 2.1, 2.4](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/finalised-guidance/fg24-1.pdf), P; see also [FCA PERG 8](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg8), P). Even posts in Reddit-style forums can count ([Lexology summary of FG24/1](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=77502a9d-7b03-4a21-b840-6917ef17e617), S).
- **Companies Act 2006 s755:** "A private company ... must not offer to the public any securities of the company" ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/section/755), P). s756 says an offer to "any section of the public, however selected" counts.
- **Public offers regime:** the Public Offers and Admissions to Trading Regulations 2024 came into force on **19 January 2026**; it is "unlawful for relevant securities to be offered to the public in the United Kingdom" unless an exemption applies ([FCA PS25/9](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/policy-statements/ps25-9-new-rules-public-offers-admissions-trading-regime), P; [POATRs reg 12](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/105), P). The gov.uk start-up note still cites FSMA s85 and is older ([gov.uk, Track 2, 2025-12-02](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/unlocking-space-for-investment-growth-hub/track-2-legal-considerations-for-raising-equity-finance), P), so treat that page as superseded on the statute number.
- **Inducements to invest are banned** for high-risk investment promotions (FCA PS22/10 "banning inducements to invest") ([FCA portfolio letter](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/correspondence/expectations-investment-based-crowdfunding-platforms-portfolio-letter.pdf), P). Pete should not run "refer a friend, get a free year if they invest" style offers.

**Vault·It must NOT (until it has an incorporated company, a regulated platform and legal advice):**
1. Offer, imply or accept money in exchange for shares, a "stake", options, convertible notes, SAFE-style instruments, a revenue/profit share, a loan, a mini-bond or any promise of financial return, on the page, in ads, in DMs, in Discord/Telegram/Reddit posts, or via influencers.
2. Use "invest in Vault·It", "become a part-owner", "founding investor", "early investor", "equity" or "returns" language. Use "Founding Member" only for a product licence and say so in plain words.
3. Describe Founding Member payments as "funding the company" with an expectation of profit, or as "donations".
4. Pay affiliates/influencers to push anything that reads as an investment invitation (FCA FG24/1 para 1.7: unauthorised influencers "may be committing a criminal offence").
5. Promise refunds, buy-backs or "guaranteed" outcomes beyond what the T&Cs say.
6. Pool money from many people with a promise they will share in profits (collective-investment-scheme risk; not researched further, see gaps).

## 4. Paid-social / search platform policy for the UK

### 4.1 Does a budgeting / net-worth tracker count as a "financial product or service"?

**Short answer: not as a regulated product, but there are three things that can pull it into scope.**

**What the FCA regime regulates** — the FCA lists regulated advertising areas as loans, investments, savings/bank accounts, insurance, pensions, mortgages, life assurance, payment services/e-money, claims management, crypto ([FCA, Regulating financial promotions and adverts](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/financial-promotions-adverts), P). A manual-entry tracker is not on that list.

**Three perimeter risks for Vault·It (judgement calls; confirm with a regulatory lawyer — I could not find a document that answers them for Vault·It specifically):**

1. **Debt counselling.** Article 39E RAO makes it a regulated activity to give "advice to a borrower about the liquidation of a debt due under a credit agreement". PERG 17.5: advice "either explicitly or implicitly steers the customer to a particular course of action"; "Any element of evaluation, value judgment or persuasion is likely to mean that advice is being given"; the medium can be "an interactive software system" (Q5.7). Info is safe if it is "balanced and neutral" (Q5.3), and a budget planner that only organises the user's own figures "is not debt counselling" (PERG 17.7 example 15), but advising "how to match income and debts" is (example 16). General website advice to the public is "unlikely to be debt counselling" (Q4.1) while advice to a particular debtor over the Internet "may be regulated" ([PERG 17.4](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg17/perg17s4), [17.5](https://www.handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/PERG/17/5.html?date=2025-04-30), [17.7](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg17/perg17s7), P).
   - **Where the repo sits (I):** the user picks Avalanche or Snowball (good), but copy such as "let Vault·It order your payoff for maximum momentum or maximum savings", "clear this first" and "Avalanche saves you £X in interest" edges toward steering. **Consequence:** show both methods side by side with figures, avoid "we recommend", keep "Not financial advice" prominent, and get a short legal opinion before running debt-led ads.
2. **Account information service.** FCA PERG 15 Q25A: AIS providers "include businesses that provide users with an on-line 'dashboard' where they can view information from various payment accounts in a single place"; "The business that requires authorisation or registration ... is the one that provides consolidated account information to the payment service user"; an agent "cannot provide or purport to provide account information services in its own right" ([FCA PERG 15](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg15), P). Registered AISPs need professional indemnity insurance ([PSRs 2017 reg 18](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2017/752/part/2/crossheading/registration-as-an-account-information-service-provider), P). The repo has a TrueLayer path. **Consequence:** do not mention bank linking in ads or on `/sign-up` until the position is confirmed.
3. **Investment-type claims.** The app tracks stocks & funds, precious metals and crypto. Any language that invites people to buy or hold specific investments is a possible s21 financial promotion. Reddit separately bans ads for "Single securities, stocks, bonds ... Penny stocks" ([Reddit policy](https://business.reddithelp.com/s/article/financial-cryptocurrency-products-and-services-policy), P). **Consequence:** talk about *tracking* holdings, never about picking or buying them.

### 4.5 What ad reviewers check on the landing page (checklist)

Combined from Meta, Google, TikTok, LinkedIn and the CAP Code. Items marked (S) rely on secondary summaries.

- Page loads on mobile, HTTPS, no 404s, no forced download, no exit pop-ups (LinkedIn: "Do not send members to a landing page that generates a pop-up"; "The domain of your Display URL must match the domain of your Destination URL" — [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/ads-policy), P; Meta/TikTok equivalents (S)).
- Offer matches ad (Meta, P).
- Business identity, contact details and address visible (CAP 3.4.2, P; gov.uk distance selling, P; Google asks for "physical address" if treated as financial, P).
- Privacy notice linked beside the form and a real web page (not a PDF) (S: [WhiteCraft, 2026-09-30](https://white-craft.com/blog/meta-ads-landing-page-requirements)); content per CAP 10.2: controller identity, purposes and legal basis, recipients, transfers outside the UK, retention period, rights, right to withdraw consent, right to complain, automated decision-making ([CAP s10](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/10.html), P).

- Refund/cancellation policy and, for crowdfunding, a plain statement of what backers get.

### 5.3 Email list and sign-up form (PECR reg 22, UK GDPR)

- **Consent needed:** "You must not send marketing emails or texts to individuals without specific consent. There is a limited exception for your own previous customers, often called the 'soft opt-in'." The soft opt-in "does not apply to prospective customers or new contacts" ([ICO, Electronic mail marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/), P; note the ICO flags this page as under review after the DUAA). Waitlist visitors are prospects, so use an unticked, specific marketing opt-in.
- **Consent standard:** UK GDPR consent, "freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous ... clear affirmative action"; no pre-ticked boxes ([CAP s10 definitions](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/10.html), P; ICO explainer at [ico.opencourtdata.uk](https://ico.opencourtdata.uk/marketing-rules-pecr-fines-2026), S). Every email needs a simple free opt-out and must not disguise the sender (CAP 10.7, 10.12).
- **Do not run "enter your friends' emails" or "forward this to friends" mechanics:** ICO "would therefore advise against this type of viral marketing" and says encouraging forwarding means you "instigate" the message ([ICO, Electronic mail marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/), P). A personal share link the user posts themselves is a different (lower-risk) pattern (I).
- **Direct messages count as electronic mail.** The same rule applies to "direct messages via social media" (same ICO page).
- **Complaints duty:** since **19 June 2026** controllers "must now give people a clear way to raise a data protection complaint, acknowledge it within 30 days, investigate appropriately and communicate the outcome" ([ICO news, 2026-06-23](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/06/new-data-protection-complaints-law-now-in-force/); [DPA 2018 s164A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2018/12/part/6/crossheading/complaints); [ICO guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/how-to-deal-with-data-protection-complaints), P). Put a complaints contact in the privacy notice.
- **Kickstarter backer data** may not be used for targeted advertising (K11 above).

## 6. Consolidated "the page / the ads must..." list

**The `/sign-up` page must**
1. Say who you are: trader name (PT Live Design / Pete Thickett), contact email, business address; link to privacy notice, terms and a complaints contact (CAP 3.4.2; gov.uk distance selling; DPA s164A).
2. Use an unticked, separate marketing opt-in with plain wording about what you will send and how often; separate from any "I accept the terms" tick (PECR reg 22; ICO consent standard).
3. Collect only email (and name if needed) at sign-up. Ask screening questions (debt, income, deposit goal) later, on your own page, never in a Meta lead form.
4. Show prices with VAT treatment, cancellation right and a refund policy if any money is taken; define "lifetime"; show a true remaining count for "33 spots" or drop it; no countdown that resets.
5. Carry no unproven testimonials; label tester quotes; disclose AI-assisted development if it is true; remove "I built every line myself" unless accurate.
6. Keep the footer line "Not regulated by the FCA. Not financial advice. Vault·It is a personal finance tracking tool only." and avoid wording that steers a user's debt repayment ("we recommend you pay X first").
7. Make no investment invitation: no equity, returns, revenue share or "invest" language anywhere.
8. Load no third-party fonts/scripts that you cannot name in the privacy notice (self-host fonts, three.js and supabase-js if possible), and carry no ad tags until a consent banner exists.
9. If analytics are used, include a short notice and a working opt-out (statistical-purposes exception).
10. Not advertise bank linking / Open Banking until the AIS position is confirmed.

**The ads must**
1. Describe the product, not the viewer's finances (no "Are you in debt?"; no amounts of debt).
2. Contain no guarantees, "get rich", unrealistic outcome claims or fabricated scarcity.
3. Match the landing page offer exactly.
4. Target 18+; declare any special ad category the platform prompts; do not try to evade classification.
5. Be labelled as ads if creator-made (CAP 2.1; ASA/CAP influencer guidance).
6. Send no financial parameters to platform pixels, and run no pixel without opt-in consent.
7. On Google, lead with deposit/net-worth benefits; keep debt-focused messaging out to protect remarketing; apply for the non-financial-services exemption if challenged.
8. Not promote the crowdfunding/pre-sale as an investment; no ads soliciting funds on LinkedIn; no giveaways without a free-entry route.
