# Lifetime ISA and stamp duty after the October Budget: the facts

**Subtitle:** What changed on 29 October, what did not, and what stamp duty costs a first-time buyer today.

The Budget on 28 October changed one Lifetime ISA rule and announced a new account for first-time buyers. Stamp duty did not change. Here are the facts in one place.

This post is facts, not a recommendation. Whether any of this suits you depends on your own situation.

## The Lifetime ISA today

- You can open one if you are 18 to 39.
- You can pay in up to £4,000 a tax year until you are 50.
- The government adds a 25% bonus, up to £1,000 a year.
- To use it for a first home, the home must cost £450,000 or less and the account must have been open for at least 12 months.

## What changed on 29 October 2026

Before: if you used your Lifetime ISA towards a first home costing more than £450,000, you paid a 25% charge. That took back the bonus and a slice of your own money too.

Now: for withdrawals on or after 29 October 2026, that charge is 20%. You still lose the bonus, and the growth on it, but you keep your own savings.

What did **not** change:

- The £450,000 price cap stays. It has not moved since the Lifetime ISA started in 2017.
- Taking money out for any other reason, apart from retirement, still costs 25%.

## What is coming: the First Time Buyer ISA

The government plans a new First Time Buyer ISA from April 2029, as reported so far:

- up to £4,000 a year
- a 25% bonus, capped at £20,000 over the life of the account
- the same £450,000 price cap
- no upper age limit to open one.

The Lifetime ISA stays open for now, and existing savers can keep paying in. Details may change before 2029.

## Stamp duty for first-time buyers (England and Northern Ireland)

No change in the Budget. The rates since 1 April 2025:

- **First-time buyers:** 0% on the first £300,000, then 5% on the part from £300,001 to £500,000.
- **Above £500,000:** no first-time buyer relief. Standard rates apply to the whole price.

What that means in pounds:

| Price | First-time buyer pays | Standard rates would be |
|---|---|---|
| £200,000 | £0 | £1,500 |
| £300,000 | £0 | £5,000 |
| £400,000 | £5,000 | £10,000 |
| £500,000 | £10,000 | £15,000 |
| £550,000 | £17,500 (no relief) | £17,500 |

Scotland and Wales have their own taxes and rules.
