# Credit control: getting paid

Goal: cash in sooner without damaging good customer relationships. Firm, polite and on schedule.

## Before the invoice

- Agree payment terms in writing before starting work. 14 or 30 days is normal.
- Invoice the day the work is done. Include: your company name and number, registered office, invoice number, date, due date, clear description, bank details, a PO number if they use them.
- For new or large clients, take a deposit (30–50%) or bill in stages.
- Offer easy payment: bank transfer details on the invoice, plus a card link (Stripe, Tide) if you can.

## Chase ladder

| When | Step | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| 3 days before due | Friendly reminder with invoice attached | Light, helpful |
| Due date +1 | "Just checking this landed": ask for the payment date | Polite |
| +7 days | Second chase: state amount and days overdue, ask who handles payments | Direct |
| +14 days | Phone call, then email confirming what was agreed | Firm |
| +30 days | Formal notice: mention statutory interest and compensation under the Late Payment Act | Formal |
| +45 days | Letter before action: 14 days to pay before court | Formal |
| +60 days | Small claims (Money Claim Online, up to £10,000) or debt recovery. Consider stopping work | Last resort |

Adjust for the relationship. For a big, valued client, call earlier and lean on the statutory steps later.

## Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998

Applies to business-to-business invoices only (not consumers).

- **Statutory interest**: 8% a year above the Bank of England base rate. Use the base rate on 31 December (for debts falling due January to June) or 30 June (for July to December). Check the current rate on the Bank of England website.
  Daily interest = amount × (8% + base rate) ÷ 365.
- **Fixed compensation** per invoice: £40 (under £1,000), £70 (£1,000 to £9,999.99), £100 (£10,000 or more).
- Reasonable extra recovery costs can be claimed too.
- Default payment term if none agreed: 30 days. Public bodies have to pay within 30 days. Business-to-business terms over 60 days must be fair.

Whether to actually charge it is a commercial call. Mentioning it at +30 days often works on its own.

## Drafting rules

- Write in the owner's voice (see profile tone notes). Short. One ask per email.
- Always include invoice number, amount, original due date, days overdue, and how to pay.
- Never threaten anything the owner won't do.
- Create Gmail drafts if connected. Never send without an explicit yes.

## Templates (adapt to the owner's voice)

**Due date +1**
> Subject: Invoice {inv} – {amount}
> Hi {name}, just checking invoice {inv} for {amount} reached you OK. It was due yesterday. Can you let me know when it's scheduled? Bank details are on the invoice. Thanks, {owner}

**+7 days**
> Subject: Invoice {inv} – now 7 days overdue
> Hi {name}, invoice {inv} for {amount} is now 7 days overdue. Can you confirm a payment date this week? If it's with someone else, let me know who to speak to. Thanks, {owner}

**+30 days (formal)**
> Subject: Overdue invoice {inv} – {amount}
> Hi {name}, invoice {inv} for {amount} was due on {due} and is now {days} days overdue. Please pay within 7 days. Under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 we're entitled to add statutory interest of {rate}% a year (currently {daily} a day) and fixed compensation of {comp}. We'd rather not, so a payment date would be appreciated. {owner}

**Letter before action (+45 days)**
> {owner company}, {address}
> {date}
> Dear {name}, invoice {inv}: {amount} due {due}. Despite reminders on {dates}, this remains unpaid. Unless we receive {total including interest and compensation} within 14 days of this letter, we intend to start court proceedings without further notice, which may add to your costs. Yours sincerely, {owner}

For letters before action over £10,000, or if the debtor disputes the work, suggest a solicitor.

## Track it

Keep a simple log (Google Sheet, or in the profile): invoice, customer, amount, due, last chase, next step, promised date. Show overdue money and average days to pay (DSO) in the Monday brief.
