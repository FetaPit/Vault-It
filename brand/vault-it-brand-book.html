Brickwork, to scale

The house is built from real 3D bricks at true proportions, laid in Flemish bond. Each brick is a solid box, so the wall has thickness you can see at every window, at the corner and along the course being laid.

Gable end · the bond continues up the rake
Brick215 × 102.5 × 65 mm, drawn as 2.87 × 1.37 × 0.87 units
Joint10 mm, shown as a dark mortar line in Night and white in Daylight
Course75 mm. 1 unit. 154 courses reach the top of the chimney, 11.55 m
BondFlemish: stretcher, header, repeating. Every header sits over the middle of the stretcher below it
CornersLong and short returns swap every course, so corners interlock like real quoins
Roof taperThe gable keeps the same bond grid as the wall below. The last brick on each course is cut to the roof line, so nothing floats or shifts
OpeningsBricks are cut around the door and windows. Stone sills and lintels sit below and above, then frames and glass arrive
Wall225 mm solid wall. Headers reach through the wall, stretchers sit on the face
RoofOverlapping slabs laid eaves to ridge on each slope
LayingBrick by brick. A deposit buys an exact number of bricks and each one is placed in order along the current course. The ring closes as the course completes. A new course only starts when the last one is full
Brick valueNot fixed. It scales with the property price and the deposit % set: deposit target × 77% ÷ 14,465 bricks. The other 23% of the deposit pays for the tiles, glass and finish. Change the price or the percentage and every brick changes value, and the house re-lays to match what is already saved
Last brickThe most recently laid brick is lit bright gold, slightly proud of the wall, with a ring and its number (BRICK #7,476). The gold slice sits on its course, so "ROW n / 154" is always the course being laid

Logo

A vault dial, a V, one brick.

The icon is the vault door and the V in one mark. Refined from the app version: fewer parts, one geometry, three sizes.

Primary icon

Teal ring, four locking bolts, a regular tick ring and a V on a dark halo. The gold brick at the pivot is the one point of gold, marking where you are.

What changed from the app version

  • Eight small ring nodes removed. Four locking bolts stay at the compass points.
  • Tick ring rebuilt: 60 even ticks, 12 longer ones, true radial symmetry.
  • V rebuilt thinner on one geometry: equal arms, flat-cut sharp tips, a two-tone fold and a fine highlight edge.
  • Halo is a hard-edged mitred outline. No rounded caps, no blobs.
  • Pivot dot becomes a small gold brick, tying the dial to the wall.
  • Glow lives in the page, not the file, so exports stay crisp.
  • Dark disc is built in, so it sits on any background.
  • Three drawn sizes instead of one design scaled down.

Daylight

A light mode that is still the future.

For people who struggle with dark screens. Bright text on black can blur, smear or ghost. Daylight swaps neon on night for deep teal ink on soft paper, keeps every colour in its place and draws the 3D house as a blueprint.

Why it exists
  • Dark mode is a choice, never the only option.
  • Dark text on a light ground is easier to read for many people with astigmatism and for long sessions.
  • Money screens carry numbers. Numbers need contrast, not glow.
How it stays on brand
  • Same hues as Night, just darker. Teal still builds, gold still means now, red is still owed.
  • Blueprint grid replaces scanlines. Hairline rules and corner brackets replace bloom.
  • The house reads as a blueprint: deep teal brick, white mortar, amber slice.
  • Same icons, same beat, same feedback.

Iconography

One grid, one stroke, one colour code.

Version 2: the same icons and ids, redrawn with more detail and a lit core so each one reads at a glance. Colour says where an icon belongs. Shape says what it is. Built means lit.

Anatomy
  • 24 grid, 2 unit safe margin.
  • 1.1 hairline stroke, 1.5 under 24px.
  • 14% body fill of the same colour under the stroke.
  • 42% lit core on the one part that names the icon.
  • 0.6 detail lines at 55%: ticks, seams, rivets, HUD brackets.
  • A solid signal node on the active point. Corners are chamfered, not rounded.
  • One colour per icon, taken from the code below.
Rules
  • Same glyph, different colour is allowed: Home is coral for rent, teal for rental income.
  • Never colour by taste. Every colour maps to a domain, a spending bucket or a status.
  • No text characters as icons. Use the pack instead of ✎ ✕ ＋ ◈ ✓ ↩.
  • Never draw an icon with a gradient or a second hue.
  • Detail lines drop out below 24px. The lit core and the nodes stay, so small icons keep their identity.
  • Commitment reads in the lock: lit and shut is committed, open and unlit is variable.

Weight

A 1.1 hairline leaves room for ticks, dials and rivets, which is what gives the set its tech feel. Version 2 adds a lit core and signal nodes, so shapes stay distinct when the detail drops out. Below 24px the line steps up to 1.5 and the detail lines go.
