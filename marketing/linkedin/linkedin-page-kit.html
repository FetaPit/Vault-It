03 · About

Overview

Overview
Vault·It shows you the way from debt to your own front door.

Put your numbers in. Your debts, your savings, your deposit. Vault·It turns every pound you save into bricks on a 3D house and gives you a keys date. No bank login. You type the numbers in and you stay in control.

Why it exists
Most people in debt have no date for owning a home. The average first-time buyer is now 34. New StepChange clients owe £16,874 on average. A goal ten years away feels unreal, especially if your brain finds distant goals hard to hold onto. So we made it something you can see, and build, one brick at a time.

Debt, then deposit, then keys.

Who it's for
People paying off debt who want to buy a home. And the building societies, brokers and debt charities who help them, through shared dashboards and white label.

Where we are
Live in alpha. Self-funded and built in Lincoln by Pete Thickett, designer and founder of PT Live Design. Accepted into Web Summit's startup programme and supported by the Business Lincolnshire Growth Hub.

One More Brick

Post title
The thing I needed didn't exist, so I built it
Post subtitle
Why I'm building Vault·It, and why I'm going to write about it as I go
Post body
Someone told me once that authors write the books they needed themselves.

I think about that a lot.

James Clear did it with Atomic Habits. He got badly hurt, rebuilt himself one small habit at a time, then wrote down what worked. There's a line in it I keep coming back to: "You do not rise to the level of your goals. You fall to the level of your systems."

I didn't write a book. I built an app. But it started the same way.

Where it started

I was saving, or trying to. But I couldn't see where any of it was going or what I actually owned. Buying a house felt like this far-off thing with no date on it. Something other people did.

I didn't want another spreadsheet. I wanted to see it. I wanted to know what I was working towards, and exactly how much I needed to put in to lay one more brick.

So I built that.

What Vault·It does

You put your numbers in. What you owe, what you've saved, what you need for a deposit. No bank login, you just type it in.

Every pound you save becomes a brick on a 3D house. You watch it go up row by row. And it gives you a date for your keys. Pay off a card and the date comes closer. Miss a month and you can see that too.

Debt, then deposit, then keys. That's the whole idea.

Why it matters

The average first-time buyer is now 34. In the 1990s it was 29. House prices are 7.6 times average earnings. New StepChange clients owe £16,874 on average before they've even started.

Loads of people want a home. Most of them don't have a date. Without a date it's just a wish.

And if your brain struggles with things that are a long way off, a ten-year goal may as well not exist. Time-blindness is real. So I'd rather put the goal right in front of you, where you can spin it round and see the gap.

A bit about me

I'm a designer from Lincoln. I studied architecture, spent years in brand, interactive and 3D work, and led visual comms in a Ministry of Defence innovation team. I run a small studio, PT Live Design.

I'm not a banker. I'm someone who needed this and couldn't find it.

Where it's at

Vault·It is live in alpha. It's self-funded and I've built it myself. 26 people have saved vaults so far, and the feedback has been brilliant. Not always nice, but that's what I need.

This Saturday I'm pitching it in London at Eligent Club. First time in front of investors with it. I'll let you know how it goes.

Why I'm writing this

I want to write the build down as it happens. The wins, the rubbish bits, what I learn about money, design and starting something from scratch. Partly to keep myself on track. Partly because someone else might be at the start of the same road.

If that's you, subscribe and come along.

And if you want to try it, the beta's free at vault-it.co. Tell me what you think. I read everything.

Pete
